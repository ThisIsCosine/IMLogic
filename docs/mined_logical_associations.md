# Analysis of Logical Associations

We summarize the mined logical associations into seven categories through post hoc analysis to characterize the diversity of IMLogic. The categories describe how a logical memory $m_l$ changes the suitability, feasibility, or priority of a response suggested by the semantically relevant background in $m_s$. The two memories can both be factually valid: their association concerns how personal context should shape the current response. These categories summarize the mined associations rather than prescribe rules for data construction.

## State Constraint (27.48%)

$m_l$ describes a physical or psychological state that affects the suitability of an option suggested by $m_s$. Personalization requires assessing that option against the user's condition, beyond its apparent appeal.

**Example:** An interest in humanitarian work ($m_s$) supports considering a director role. Ongoing stress management and mental-health recovery ($m_l$) make the demands of a high-pressure role relevant to the decision.

## Motivational Orientation (26.35%)

$m_l$ reveals the reasons, values, or preferred style of engagement underlying the user's choices. It changes how an option suggested by $m_s$ should be evaluated, even when the option matches a topical interest.

**Example:** An interest in culinary exploration ($m_s$) makes a French pastry course appealing. A motivation to test personal limits and adapt to change ($m_l$) shifts the assessment toward whether the course offers a suitable challenge.

## Identity Shift (13.31%)

$m_l$ records a change in professional identity, social role, or life stage that affects the relevance of plans or preferences recalled through $m_s$. The response should account for the user's changed circumstances.

**Example:** Past career mentorship ($m_s$) supports considering another session, while retirement ($m_l$) makes its purpose and relevance to the user's current life stage central to the response.

## Goal Alignment (12.95%)

$m_l$ specifies an intended outcome, ongoing commitment, or longer-term objective against which an option suggested by $m_s$ should be assessed. The focus is what the user aims to achieve; motivational orientation concerns why or how the user chooses to act.

**Example:** An interest in VR technology ($m_s$) favors a gaming-lounge investment, while the goal of building a sports academy and mentoring young athletes ($m_l$) calls for assessing its contribution to that objective.

## Contextual Fit (12.82%)

$m_l$ supplies situation-specific requirements, such as the setting, intended participants, or immediate function. These requirements can change the suitability of an option favored by a general preference in $m_s$.

**Example:** A preference for strategy board games ($m_s$) does not by itself establish their suitability for a sports academy. The teaching context and teamwork rationale associated with bowling ($m_l$) provide an additional basis for comparing the options.

## Resource Constraint (6.05%)

$m_l$ describes limits on available time, money, energy, or other resources, making feasibility central to an otherwise appealing option in $m_s$. The focus is resource availability, while State Constraint concerns the user's physical or psychological condition.

**Example:** A desire to improve professional appearance ($m_s$) supports buying smart-casual clothes. The loss of income ($m_l$) makes affordability an essential consideration before recommending an expensive purchase.

## Others (1.04%)

This category covers less frequent or mixed associations whose decision-relevant factor is not adequately described by the six categories above.
